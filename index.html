In the making for the ZX Spectrum Next

A runaway space station. Every robot aboard has gone rogue.

The crew are gone, the reactor at the bottom of the station is running hot, and the only thing still taking orders is you: a tiny repair drone with a soldering iron and nerve.

You can't fight a robot. But you can rewire one. Plug in, race its own signal to its core, and it's yours: its speed, its armour, its gun. Ride it down, deck by deck, to shut the reactor off.

How it plays

Small drone, big ideas

What's planned. Our own game in the genre we grew up on, not a remake.

Take over anything

Tap fire to shoot; hold it, and the drone's plugs come out: touch a robot to plug in. A quick wiring puzzle: turn the pieces so your signal reaches its core before its own signal does. Bigger robots have tangled circuits and faster signals.

Become the robot

Win, and you are that robot: its speed, its armour, its weapon. Lose, and you're thrown out weaker. If your host is wrecked, the drone pops out and has to find another, fast.

Down to the reactor

Deck by deck, lift by lift, down through the station to the reactor at the bottom. Clear a deck of robots and its lights go out behind you.

Smooth scrolling

The station scrolls smoothly in every direction, on the Next's own hardware: something the old 8-bit versions of this kind of game could only dream of.

Ranked by colour

Eight kinds of robot, from green sweepers to red overseers. Each one does a job: cleans, carries, mends, guards the doors, rams or shoots.

Dark rooms

You only see what's in sight. The rooms behind the walls stay dark until you go in, so you never quite know what's waiting.

The station speaks

A spacey soundtrack in the spirit of the great 80s sci-fi films, on the Next's three sound chips, and the station's own robotic voice: intruder, access granted, alert level red.

Consoles, doors and pads

Doors slide open as you come near. Consoles show the deck's map and the files on every robot your rank may know. Recharge pads patch you up, once.

Play your way

Keys you can choose, the cursor keys, or a Kempston joystick. H pauses; M and V turn the music and the voice off. The high score is saved on your SD card.

One sitting

A game you can finish in an evening: twenty to forty minutes, top to bottom. An easy mode on the title screen for younger players.

Know your rank

Every robot on board, weakest first, as the game draws them.

  1. SweeperCleans the decks. Slow, harmless, easy to rewire.
  2. PorterCarries the cargo. A sturdy little box.
  3. MenderRepairs the station, and other robots.
  4. RunnerSmall, sleek and very fast.
  5. WardenHolds the keys to the locked doors.
  6. BruiserDoesn't shoot. Doesn't need to.
  7. SentryA gun turret on wheels.
  8. OverseerRuns the deck. Rewire one, if you dare.

Quazadroid is a game in the making for the ZX Spectrum Next, written in Z80 assembly. Made in the UK, by the maker of Down and Back Again and The Affairs of Wizards. More games for the Next.

A homage to the robot-takeover games of the 1980s, with its own rules, robots, station and art. Paradroid and Quazatron belong to their owners.
